The quiz, open

"What brought you here today?" sits on every screen as a small pill and opens to eight plain answers, from "tired or foggy" to "comparing brands". Each answer routes to one place to start. No email needed.

Decision

Ask one question, not ten.

Why: a full quiz is a commitment; one tap is curiosity. It catches the visitor who would otherwise bounce off a shelf they cannot read yet.

TTest

Twelve tests. Five wins.

Every change shipped behind a hypothesis and one primary metric. Twelve A/B tests ran over five months at a 42% win rate, and the homepage was where the new architecture was proven first.

  • First-click tests picked the goal-led IA before anything was built.
  • The losers were written up and fed the next round.

EEmpathise

Every page drew its own buttons

The audits in Looks 01 to 03 kept finding the same thing: price lines, badges, trust rows and buttons drawn slightly differently on every page. Developers rebuilt the same block several ways, and every rebuild drifted.

  • One designer cannot review every pixel of 300+ screens.
  • Engineering needed rules, not redlines.
  • The brand had to read as scientific and premium, never as a generic Shopify store or a vitamin marketplace.

DDefine

Colour means one thing

Five brand values, no more. Everything else is a tint, a shade or a semantic alias, and the semantics stay narrow: green for success, amber for warning, red for error, Public Blue for info. No purple, no teal, no lime.

Decision

Name tokens by role, never by hue.

Why: "blue-600" tells a developer nothing; "color-primary" tells them where it goes and where it never goes. Trade-off: more names to learn, fewer wrong choices.

Decision

One signature, used with discipline.

The chamfer is a 45 degree cut at 0.35 times the control height, taken from the master button artwork. Why: the brand lives inside the components, not in decoration. Everything else gets a quiet 4 px radius.
